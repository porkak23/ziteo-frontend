## Design Context

### Users
Construction professionals in Bolivia (initially only in Sucre, Potosi, and Santa Cruz, other cities disabled until expansion): contractors (constructores), material suppliers (proveedores), skilled tradespeople (maestros), and delivery drivers (choferes). Used in both office settings (reviewing projects, orders, analytics) and in the field (looking up contracts, ordering materials quickly). Professional users who expect efficiency and reliability from a work tool — not casual consumers.

### Brand Personality
**Moderno · Ágil · Ambicioso.** A tech startup transforming a traditional industry. The interface should feel like a high-caliber professional tool — not a generic marketplace. Closest spirit: Linear / Notion — polished, direct, clear visual personality.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Referents**: Linear / Notion — clean, strong typography, high information density without visual noise
- **Theme**: Full light + dark support, system-preference following. Both modes first-class.
- **Visual tone**: Tool-first, not marketplace-first. Less color, more hierarchy. Orange as brand accent only where attention matters — CTAs, active states, key data.
- **Anti-referents**: Rappi, PedidosYa (too noisy), Mercado Libre (no character)
- **Fonts**: Space Grotesk (500/600/700, display headlines → `font-headline`) + Manrope (400–800, body and labels → `font-body`/`font-label`)
- **Primary color**: #A43700 (orangeDark — filled CTAs/buttons) with #E8733A (orange — accent, active states, gradient start). Source of truth: the Claude Design handoff `Z` tokens. The older #D94F00 is deprecated and fully removed.

### Design Principles
1. **Information first**: Data is the product. UI is the vessel.
2. **Trust through precision**: Consistent spacing, clear typography, predictable states.
3. **Orange is a tool, not decoration**: Only where attention matters — CTAs, active states, key data.
4. **Context-adaptive**: Light in the office, dark on the job site. Both modes first-class.
5. **Bolivia first**: Authentically local UX (currently limited to Sucre, Potosi, and Santa Cruz), not a Silicon Valley translation.
